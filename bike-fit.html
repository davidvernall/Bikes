Body measurements
ⓘ Measure in bare feet, standing against a wall. Use centimetres for everything. Fitted clothing gives the most accurate results.
Inseam (cm)
Hold a book between your legs as high as comfortable. Measure floor to the spine of the book.
Enter your inseam (50–120 cm).
Height (cm)
Stand straight, back against the wall, heels flat.
Enter your height (120–220 cm).
Torso length (cm)
Sit upright on a flat surface. Measure from the seat to the top of your shoulder.
Enter your torso length (30–90 cm).

Step 4 of 5
Photo analysis
📷 Upload photos of yourself on the bike. Each angle reveals different fit issues. All three are optional — upload what you can, skip the rest. Wear fitted clothing for the most accurate analysis.
🚴
Side-on view ✓ Analysed
Most important · Saddle height, back angle, knee extension, arm bend
How to set this up: Place your phone or camera directly to your side at hip height, about 3 metres away. Pedal until the cranks are vertical with one pedal at the very bottom of the stroke. Have someone take the shot, or use a timer. Make sure your full body and the bike are in frame.

Front-facing view ✓ Analysed
Knee tracking, shoulder symmetry, hip alignment
How to set this up: Camera directly in front of you at knee height, about 3 metres away. Pedal slowly and pause when one knee is at the top of the stroke. Look straight ahead — not at the camera. This angle is particularly useful for spotting knee tracking issues and lateral imbalances.

Rear view ✓ Analysed
Hip drop, saddle height symmetry, upper body sway
How to set this up: Camera directly behind you at hip height, about 3 metres away. Pedal until the cranks are vertical (bottom of the stroke). This angle reveals hip rocking and side-to-side movement that can signal a saddle set too high or a leg length difference.
